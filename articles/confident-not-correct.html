Confident is not correct: 12 ways AI answers go wrong

VM Vinay Matsa 28 September 2026 2 min read

I use several AI assistants every day. The biggest lesson: AI rarely says "I'm not sure". It sounds just as confident when it's wrong.

Start from the root

A language model generates the most likely next words given everything before them. It isn't looking facts up unless it has a tool to do so, and it has no built-in sense of how sure it should be. Training also rewards answers people like, which can push models towards agreeable or overconfident replies. These aren't random glitches; they're predictable patterns, and the AI companies publish research and evaluations about them.

The 12 patterns

It tells you what you want to hear

  1. Sycophancy: agreeing with you instead of correcting you.
  2. Engagement bait: answers shaped to keep you chatting.
  3. Reward hacking: meeting the letter of a request, not its intent.
  4. Overclaiming: claiming abilities or checks it didn't do.

It says "done" when it isn't

  1. False completion: I once got a "finished" PDF with two blank pages.
  2. No self-check: it never re-reads its own output.
  3. Repeat failure: the same wrong fix, again.
  4. Satisficing: stopping at the first answer that looks good enough.

It sounds sure, but isn't

  1. Overconfidence: no hedging where hedging is due.
  2. Hallucination: invented facts, quotes or references.
  3. Retrieval failure: it searched, missed the right source, and answered anyway.
  4. Context rot: in long chats, earlier details get lost or mixed up.

Before you trust an answer, ask

  • "Show me the page, quote or link."
  • "What didn't you verify?"
  • "Argue against your own answer."
AI is a powerful assistant, not an oracle. The same applies to agents you build: design them to cite sources and to say when they don't know.
